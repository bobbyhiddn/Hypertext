A luminous cinematic oil painting, impressionistic brushwork, of a four-player card game in
progress, seen from a high three-quarter angle looking down across a dark walnut table. Rich
saturated blues and golds, deep shadowed edges, one warm hanging lamp above the table.

No faces and no people above the chest. Only hands, wrists and forearms enter the frame from the
four table edges. No heads, no chins, no faces anywhere.

NONE OF THE WORDS IN THESE INSTRUCTIONS MAY BE PRINTED ON ANY CARD. Do not letter a card with any
phrase from this description. Where a card has a title, it is a single short invented word in the
manner of GENESIS, COVENANT, EXODUS, REMNANT - one word, nothing else. All other lettering on the
cards is suggested texture: fine closely-spaced marks that read as rows of print at a glance and
resolve into nothing.

Scale matters: the cards are small, ordinary playing-card size against adult hands, about as long
as a hand is wide. The table is broad and mostly bare polished wood, the cards gathered in small
tidy groups with generous empty space between them, so the grain, the pooled lamplight and the
whiskey have room to breathe.

Two different kinds of card lie on the table and they must look different from each other.

The first kind has a small painting on it: a tall portrait card, navy border with fine gold
filigree, cream parchment interior, a short wide landscape picture panel near the top in a thin
gold frame, then three short rows of tiny round dark dots, then three or four narrow horizontal
panels densely filled with small dark marks, and a navy bar at the foot.

The second kind has no painting anywhere on it - that absence is the clearest difference between
the two. It is the same tall portrait shape and the same navy-and-parchment styling, but instead
of a picture it carries: a tiny dark label in the top-left corner and a small boxed label in the
top-right, a one-word title, a broad ribbon of deep navy stretched across the upper middle with
pale lettering on it and pointed ends, a line of italic text beneath, then a framed panel holding
a horizontal row of five to seven small square outline boxes side by side, each with a tiny dark
icon inside and a small word under it, joined by little plus signs, and at the foot a
dark-bordered box of dense marks. It should read as a recipe or a form, never as an illustrated card.

Face-down cards show the navy back: a gold filigree frame around a central gold monogram.

On the table, small and tidy: a modest face-down draw pile at the centre with a small face-up pile
beside it; one pictureless recipe-style card face-up near the centre and one in front of each seat;
each seat with a small fan of cards in one hand and a few gold coin tokens; the near-left player
lowering a single picture-style card face-up onto the wood, mid-motion, its little painting
catching the lamplight; and one seat with three picture-style cards laid face-up in a neat row.

A cut-crystal tumbler of amber whiskey beside three of the four seats, one holding a large ice
cube. A stoppered decanter at the far edge, half in shadow.

Warm, absorbed, unhurried - a serious game between friends, late in the evening. No text overlays,
no title, no logo, no watermark, no UI.
